Part one · Features 1–28

What the app should be able to do

The app records a great deal and asks you to type all of it. These are the things that would change that.

Group 01 · Features 1–6

Foundation & safety

Blocker

None of these are features anyone asks for. All of them are the reason the rest can exist.

1

Log in with an account

The app has no login at all. Add sign-in, so the app knows who you are and only shows your data.

Today: no auth layer anywhere in the codebase.

What changes for you

Your data becomes yours. You can open the app on a new phone and everything is there, instead of being tied to whoever happens to open the link.

2

Lock the database

The security rules on every table are currently set to "allow everyone." Anyone who finds the web address can read and change everything in it.

Every RLS policy reads USING (true) with the public key shipped to the browser.

What changes for you

Your salary, your net worth, your health logs and your vet bills stop being publicly readable by anyone who stumbles across the URL.

3

Replace the leaked keys

Three secret keys are written directly into a file that ships with the code, including one that lets anyone send notifications to your phone. Generate new ones and store them properly.

Supabase URL, anon key and the VAPID private key are all in wrangler.jsonc.

What changes for you

Nobody else can push fake notifications to your lock screen or quietly read your database in the background.

4

Keep working without internet

Save entries on the phone when there's no signal and send them up automatically when there is.

The service worker handles push only — no caching, no background sync.

What changes for you

You can log a set in a gym basement or an expense on the metro. The entry saves, and syncs itself later — you never lose one to a dead signal again.

5

Back up and download everything

Automatic backups, plus a button that gives you your entire history as a file you can keep.

What changes for you

Years of daily logging stop being one bad database change away from gone. And if you ever want to leave, you can take it all with you.

Group 02 · Features 7–14

Faster logging

Do first

Apps like this don't fail because they're missing features. They fail because entering the data becomes a chore. This is the most important group in the document.

6

Type one line to log anything

One box on the home screen. Type "petrol 1200 shell odo 42150" and the app files it as a fuel entry, an expense and an odometer reading — all at once, no forms.

What changes for you

What used to be three modules and two minutes becomes one sentence and five seconds. This is the single biggest change on the list.

7

Say it instead of typing it

Hold a button, speak the entry, done.

What changes for you

You can log a set between reps, a thought while driving, or a grocery total while carrying the bags.

8

Pull in data from your watch and phone

Connect Apple Health or Google Fit for sleep, steps, heart rate and weight.

What changes for you

Sleep starts being tracked without you doing anything — and sleep turns out to explain more of your habit and mood patterns than almost anything else.

9

Remember what you usually enter

Pre-fill the fields with your last values, and offer a "same as yesterday" button.

What changes for you

Your regular coffee, your usual commute, your standard breakfast — one tap instead of a filled-in form.

Group 03 · Features 15–18

Joining the modules up

Do first

Your roadmap treats this as a feature. It's really the plumbing that groups 04 and 05 need in order to be built at all.

10

One shared record of everything that happens

Instead of fifteen modules each keeping their own separate history, everything also writes to one common list: what happened, when, in which area, and what it cost.

What changes for you

You won't see this directly, but it's what makes "why did I save less in July?" answerable. Without it, every insight has to be hand-built one at a time.

11

A single page for every thing you own or care for

Your dog, your bike, your fridge, a project — each gets one page showing its whole story: everything spent on it, everything done to it, everything coming up.

What changes for you

Today your dog exists in four separate places and you can't answer "what has this dog cost me?" After this, one page answers it.

12

Tag anything, search everything

One search box that looks across all modules at once, plus tags that work everywhere.

What changes for you

You stop needing to remember which module a thing lives in. You just search for it.

13

Undo, and a recycle bin

Deletions go to a bin for thirty days instead of vanishing, and recent actions can be reversed.

What changes for you

A mis-tap stops being permanent. You can be quick and careless with entry, which is exactly what you want in an app you use twenty times a day.

Group 04 · Features 19–23

Planning ahead

Then

The app is a very good rear-view mirror. It currently has no windscreen.

14

Goals that connect to your daily logs

Set a yearly goal, break it into quarters and months, and link the habits, tasks and savings that feed it — so progress is measured from what you already log.

What changes for you

Your daily ticking starts adding up to something. You can see that this month's habits actually moved the year's goal.

15

One calendar for everything

Workouts, task deadlines, bills, vehicle renewals and pet appointments on a single month view — and you can drag things to reschedule, not just look at them.

What changes for you

You see next week is overloaded before you're inside it, and you can move something there and then.

16

A guided weekly and monthly review

A short, structured screen: what went well, what slipped, what you're committing to next. Not a blank page — actual questions with your own numbers already filled in.

What changes for you

Sunday evening gets a fifteen-minute ritual. This is the thing that keeps people using apps like this for years rather than months.

Group 05 · Features 24–28

Insight & AI

Then

Your existing AI roadmap, reordered. The order is the recommendation: nothing here works well on half-logged data.

17

Tell people how sure the number is

Every score and chart shows how much data it's based on. "Consistency 82% — from 19 of 30 days logged."

What changes for you

You can trust the numbers, because the app admits when it doesn't know. Without this, one wrong-looking score and you stop believing all of them.

18

Flag anything unusual

Simple, reliable alerts when something is well outside your normal range — "grocery spend is far above your six-month average."

What changes for you

You catch a drifting budget or a slipping habit in week one instead of at the end of the month.

19

A single Life Score

One 0–100 number combining habits, money, fitness, tasks and upkeep — with the formula shown, weights you can change, and hidden when there isn't enough data to be honest.

What changes for you

One glance tells you whether this week is going well. And because you set the weights, it measures your definition of a good week, not someone else's.

20

Ask questions in plain English

Type "why did I spend more in July?" and get a written answer with the chart that supports it.

What changes for you

You stop hunting through eleven report tabs for the one number you wanted. You just ask.

Group 06 · Features 29–32

Reminders that work

Do first

Your notification roadmap can't be built yet, because the piece it depends on doesn't exist.

21

Something that sends notifications on a schedule

Right now notifications can only be fired by hand from the settings screen. There's no timer, no rules, nothing that runs on its own. This builds that.

What changes for you

Nothing visible on its own — but it's the reason every reminder below can exist. Without it, the app can only speak when you're already looking at it.

22

A morning brief

One notification at 7am: today's habits, what's due, what's on the calendar, what's left in the budget, anything expiring soon.

What changes for you

You stop having to remember to check. One message a day tells you what today needs — the highest-value screen in the whole product.

23

Streak rescue and quiet hours

A nudge before a streak breaks, and controls so notifications don't arrive at 11pm or arrive six at a time.

What changes for you

The app protects the thing you've invested in without becoming the app you mute.

Later

Group 08 · Features 39–43

More than one person

Later

Everything above assumes one user. This group is the cost of a second — only worth paying if this goes beyond you.

24

Household accounts

More than one person, with control over what each can see.

What changes for you

Your partner logs the groceries and the vet visit without seeing your salary. The shared car has one fuel log instead of two half-accurate ones.

25

Let other tools connect

A basic API and webhooks so automations can read and write.

What changes for you

You can wire the app into whatever else you use rather than doing the joining by hand.

Group 09 · Features 44–46

What to drop

Cut

These are already on your roadmap. Saying no to them is what creates room for the rest.

26

The game engine — postpone it

XP, levels, a rewards shop and monthly boss battles. It's a way of making yourself want to log. But wanting to log isn't the problem — logging being slow is.

Why this helps you

Fix the speed first (features 7–14). If you're still skipping days after that, build this — and you'll know it's the right fix rather than guessing.

27

Five report timeframes — make it two

Daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly and yearly reports is five separate things to build and maintain. Ship weekly and monthly, see if they get read.

Why this helps you

Two reports you actually open beat five you don't. And the weeks saved go into the morning brief instead.

28

Stop adding report tabs

There are already eleven. The issue isn't that a report is missing — it's that reports wait for you to visit them, and you don't.

Why this helps you

Insights come to you in the morning brief instead of sitting in a tab you'd have to remember to open.

Part two · Features 29–63

Making the app easier to use

Same app, same data. These change how it feels to open it twenty times a day.

Group 10 · Features 47–51

Text, weight & colour

Do first

The heaviest possible text weight is used 2,388 times, and text at 9–11px appears 1,296 times. When everything is bold and tiny, nothing stands out.

29

Pick five text sizes and stick to them

Right now sizes are chosen at random throughout the app, and a lot of them are 9 or 10 pixels — smaller than the small print on a contract. Settle on roughly five sizes and retire everything below 12.

What changes for you

You can read the app without bringing it closer to your face. Outdoors, tired, at arm’s length — it still works.

30

Stop making everything bold

Use three weights — normal for text, semibold for emphasis, heavy for the one thing that matters most on a screen. Currently the heaviest weight is the default.

What changes for you

An overdue insurance renewal stops looking identical to a section heading. Your eye lands on the important thing instead of reading everything equally.

31

Drop the tiny ALL-CAPS labels

Wide-spaced capitals at 9 pixels appear 433 times. Capitals remove the shape of a word, which is what your eye reads first, so they’re the slowest text there is.

What changes for you

Labels become something you glance at rather than decipher. Screens get quicker to scan without losing any information.

32

Use one colour system

The app defines proper colours for success, warning and danger, then ignores them — 134 places use hardcoded colours instead.

What changes for you

Red always means the same thing everywhere. And when dark mode arrives, nothing is left glowing white in the corner of a dark screen.

33

Make all charts look the same

One shared set of colours, fonts and spacing for every graph.

What changes for you

You learn to read one chart style instead of eleven. Comparing two reports stops requiring a mental adjustment.

Group 11 · Features 52–57

Getting around the app

Do first

Sixteen modules and around 110 screens all live behind one menu button in the top-left — the hardest place on a phone for a thumb to reach.

34

A row of tabs along the bottom

Four main destinations plus a big centre button for logging, fixed at the bottom of the screen where your thumb already is. Keep the side menu for everything else.

What changes for you

Logging an expense goes from four taps and a stretch to one tap where your hand already rests.

35

Remove the Forward button

Every page shows Home, Back and Forward. A forward button is a web-browser control, not an app one, and the three of them eat about a third of the header on a phone.

What changes for you

Page titles stop being squeezed and cut off, and there’s one less button that does something you never wanted.

36

Put the header to better use

The app logo sits in the middle of every screen. In an app you installed yourself, you already know which app it is. Use that space for the page title and search.

What changes for you

You can always tell where you are, and search is one tap away from anywhere.

37

Type-to-jump shortcut

One box that finds any screen, entry or record by name.

What changes for you

Two letters gets you to fuel logs. With 110 screens, this is the single most useful navigation change on the list.

38

One tab style, and fix the Reports tabs

There are two different tab components doing the same job, styled differently. Also, Reports has eleven tabs that scroll sideways — anything off-screen may as well not exist.

What changes for you

Tabs behave the same everywhere, and you can see all your reports at once instead of discovering them by accident.

39

A proper layout on a computer

On a wide screen the app stretches the frame but keeps content in a narrow phone-width column, leaving large empty margins. Either build a real multi-column layout or stop pretending.

What changes for you

On a laptop you see several panels at once instead of scrolling through a phone screen surrounded by grey.

Group 12 · Features 58–62

The forms you use daily

Do first

Every entry currently means leaving the page you were on and filling in a full-screen form. This is the app’s busiest surface by far.

40

Forms that slide up over the page

Log a habit, expense or set in a panel that appears over what you were looking at, instead of navigating away and back.

What changes for you

You never lose your place. Log three things in a row without re-finding where you were each time.

41

Save instantly, with an undo

Show the entry as saved immediately and finish the actual saving in the background. Put an Undo button on the confirmation.

What changes for you

No waiting after every tap. And a mistake takes one tap to reverse rather than a hunt through a list.

42

Stop the screen zooming when you tap a field

The stylesheet forces every input to 13 pixels. iPhones automatically zoom in on any field smaller than 16, so the page jumps every single time you tap one.

One line in globals.css: inputs are pinned to 13px !important.

What changes for you

The lurch you get twenty times a day disappears. It’s a small thing that happens constantly, and it’s a real part of why logging feels like work.

43

Number pad and quick-pick buttons

Amount and weight fields should open a number keypad, with buttons for your recent values.

What changes for you

Entering ₹1,200 stops involving a full letter keyboard. Common amounts become one tap.

44

Don’t lose half-finished entries

Save what’s been typed so switching apps mid-form doesn’t wipe it.

What changes for you

A phone call in the middle of logging a workout no longer costs you the workout.

Group 13 · Features 63–67

Speed & feedback

Then

The app doesn’t feel slow because it is slow. It feels slow because of what it shows you while it waits.

45

Remove the fade-in on every page

Content is deliberately delayed by roughly a third of a second every time you open a screen, and the rule is broad enough to fade things that weren’t meant to fade.

What changes for you

The app becomes noticeably faster without a single query being optimised. You pay that delay hundreds of times a week today.

46

Show the shape of the page while it loads

Instead of blanking the whole screen behind a spinner, show grey placeholder blocks in the layout the content will fill. Twenty screens currently go completely empty.

What changes for you

No more white flash on every tap. You see where things will be before they arrive, which makes the wait feel much shorter than it is.

47

Show yesterday’s data straight away

Display what was there last time immediately, then quietly update it.

What changes for you

Opening the app is instant. The numbers are already there and correct themselves a moment later if anything changed.

48

Move the save message out of the middle of the screen

Confirmation messages are pinned dead centre with a bouncing animation, covering the content, every time you save.

What changes for you

Saving stops interrupting you. The message appears near the bottom, out of the way, with an Undo button on it.

Group 14 · Features 68–72

Feeling like a real app

Then

It’s installed on a home screen but still behaves like a website in a frame.

49

Keep content clear of the notch and the home bar

There’s no handling for the cut-out at the top or the bar at the bottom of modern phones, so content can slide underneath both.

What changes for you

Nothing hides behind the camera notch, and the bottom button isn’t half-covered by the home indicator.

50

Fix the dark flash when it opens

The app tells the phone its colour is near-black, then loads a near-white interface. Every launch is a dark screen followed by a white one.

What changes for you

It opens cleanly instead of flashing at you. Cheapest polish on the list.

51

Pull down to refresh

The standard phone gesture, instead of hunting for a refresh button in the corner.

What changes for you

You refresh the way you do in every other app, without looking for a button first.

52

A small vibration when something completes

A tap of feedback when you tick a habit, save an entry or extend a streak.

What changes for you

Completing something feels finished. It sounds trivial, and it’s one of the more effective things you can do for habit-building.

53

Swipe on a row to act on it

Swipe left or right on any list item to complete, edit or delete.

What changes for you

Clearing a list of tasks becomes a few swipes rather than a series of taps into and back out of each one.

Group 15 · Features 73–76

Readability for everyone

Then

Zoom is switched off, and there are 35 accessibility labels in 70,000 lines of code.

54

Allow pinch to zoom

The app explicitly blocks zooming. Combined with 9-pixel text, that leaves no way out.

What changes for you

You can zoom in on a dense table on a bus, the way you can in every other app.

55

Darken the faint grey text

A lot of secondary text is set at under half opacity, which puts it well below the readable minimum against the pale background.

What changes for you

Captions, hints and helper text become readable in daylight instead of disappearing into the background.

56

Label the buttons and make keyboards work

Icon-only buttons need names, the menu needs to close with Escape, and form fields need properly attached labels and error messages.

What changes for you

The app works with a screen reader or a keyboard — which matters for other people now, and possibly for you in twenty years, when your logging history is far too valuable to abandon.

57

Swap emoji for proper icons

53 emoji are used as interface icons. They’re read out oddly by screen readers and look different on every device.

What changes for you

The app looks the same on your phone as on your laptop, and reads as a product rather than a spreadsheet.

Group 16 · Features 77–79

Dark mode & themes

Do first

There are 34 dark-mode styles in the entire codebase, which is to say none. The groundwork is already about ninety percent done.

58

Dark mode

A proper dark theme that follows your phone’s setting.

What changes for you

You log habits at night. Right now that means a full-brightness white screen in a dark room — which is exactly the moment you decide to do it tomorrow instead. This is about whether the last log of the day happens.

59

Remove the palette button that does nothing

There’s a large floating button near the bottom of every screen offering one theme, which is the theme you’re already using.

What changes for you

One less button covering your content and promising something it doesn’t deliver.

60

Choose your own accent colour

Once dark mode exists, a handful of real palettes costs almost nothing to add.

What changes for you

The app looks like yours. Small thing, disproportionate effect on wanting to open it.

Group 17 · Features 60–63

The home screen

Then

It’s currently nine fixed panels, one per module — arranged around how the app is built rather than what you need to know.

61

Lead with what needs attention

Put overdue, expiring and unlogged items at the top. Everything that’s fine goes below.

What changes for you

You open the app and the first thing you see is the thing you need to do — instead of scrolling past five healthy modules hunting for the one that changed.

62

One number at the top

The first screenful should answer “am I on track?” without scrolling — whether that’s the Life Score or something as plain as “3 of 7 habits, ₹4,200 left this month.”

What changes for you

A two-second check-in becomes possible. Most opens don’t need more than that.

63

Design the empty screens

There’s no pattern for what a section looks like before it has data, so new modules simply render blank boxes.

What changes for you

A section you haven’t used yet explains itself and offers a first step, rather than looking broken.
